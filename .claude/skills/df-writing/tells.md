# AI writing tells (remove every one)

Consolidated from the widely shared "dead giveaways" lists (Ruben Haseed and others) plus the tropes NLW called out on The AI Daily Brief, plus Davis's own additions. Treat as a blocklist. Add to it whenever Davis flags a new one.

## Structure tells

- Tiny dramatic fragments separated by periods to sound punchy. "Simple. Fast. Done."
- "It's not X, it's Y." / "This isn't about X. It's about Y."
- The rule of three with parallel adjectives. "Fast, reliable, and secure."
- Contrast pairs everywhere. "Not just X but Y."
- Rhetorical question then answer. "Why does this matter? Because..."
- Opening with a definition or a restatement of the question.
- Closing with a summary of what was just said.
- Closing with an offer. "Let me know if you'd like me to..."
- Closing with a rallying line. "The future is here." "The best time to start is now."
- Section headers on a short email.
- Bullet lists in personal messages.
- Bold on random phrases for emphasis.
- Emoji as bullets or section markers.
- Starting three consecutive sentences the same way.

## Self-referential and meta tells

- Clapping for itself: "And that matters." "That's the part everyone misses." "Which is exactly the point." "Here's the thing."
- "Honest assessment:" / "Honestly," / "To be honest," / "I'll be straight with you" / "Let me be clear" / "Real talk"
- "Great question." "Absolutely!" "Certainly!" "I'd be happy to."
- "As an AI" or any reference to being a model.
- "In today's fast-paced world" and any opener about the modern era.
- "It's worth noting" / "It's important to note" / "Notably"
- "Let's dive in" / "Let's break it down" / "Without further ado"
- "In conclusion" / "To sum up" / "At the end of the day"
- "I hope this helps" / "Hope this helps!"
- "Whether you're X or Y, ..."

## Vocabulary tells

- delve, delving
- leverage (as a verb)
- robust, seamless, seamlessly
- unlock, unleash, supercharge, elevate, empower
- game-changer, game-changing, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, next-level
- landscape (as in "the AI landscape")
- tapestry, testament, beacon, realm, journey
- navigate (a challenge), foster, cultivate, harness
- streamline, optimize (unless literal), synergy
- crucial, pivotal, vital, essential (stacked)
- comprehensive, holistic, multifaceted, nuanced
- ever-evolving, rapidly evolving, dynamic
- utilize (use "use")
- facilitate (use "help" or say what actually happens)
- ensure (usually "make sure" or cut)
- additionally, furthermore, moreover
- ultimately, essentially, fundamentally
- genuinely, truly, really (as intensifiers)
- straightforward
- "a wide range of" / "a variety of" / "a plethora of"
- "in order to" (use "to")
- "plays a crucial role" / "serves as"
- "not only... but also"
- "stands as" / "stands out"
- "resonate," "resonates with"
- "underscores," "highlights the importance"
- "embark on"
- "at its core"
- "the power of"
- "world of" ("the world of fitness software")
- "look no further"
- "revolutionize," "transform," "reimagine"

## Punctuation tells

- Em-dashes and en-dashes. Any of them. Use commas, periods, or parentheses.
- Colons used to set up a dramatic reveal.
- Semicolons in casual writing.
- Exclamation points in business writing.
- Scare quotes around ordinary words.
- Oxford-comma triplets stacked in every other sentence.

## Tone tells

- Hedging every claim. "may," "might," "could potentially," "in some cases."
- Over-apologizing. "I apologize for any confusion."
- Fake enthusiasm. "I'm excited to share..."
- Flattery of the reader. "As a savvy gym owner..."
- Sincerity announcements before saying something plain.
- Balanced-on-both-sides framing when the writer has a view.
- Explaining what the reader already knows (what a transfer portal is, what a CRM does).

## Email-specific tells

- "I hope this email finds you well."
- "I wanted to reach out" / "I'm reaching out" / "Just circling back" / "Just following up"
- "Per my last email"
- "Don't hesitate to reach out"
- "Looking forward to hearing from you"
- "Best regards" on a two-line note (use "Thanks," or just the name)
- Subject lines with "Quick question" or "Touching base"

## Davis's additions (grow this list)

- Any time or effort estimate the reader did not ask for.
- "Simply" before an instruction.
- "Just" as a softener.
